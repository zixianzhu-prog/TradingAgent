## 2. CAI — Caris Life Sciences

**Chokepoint:** A data network across testing, AI products and pharma services, similar to Tempus but with its own sequencing scale.

**Why it can win:** More than a million sequenced tumour cases feed both AI products and pharma services, and FDA companion-diagnostic approvals put its tests inside treatment decisions. The data asset grows with every test.

**Why now:** Q3 2026 revenue grew 27% to $275M and the quarter was profitable.

**Strongest case against:** A disclosed material weakness in internal control, a $393M term loan and dependence on a few sequencer suppliers.

**We are wrong if:**
- The material weakness is not remediated by the next annual report
- Revenue growth falls below 15%
- Pharma services revenue declines

Sources: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2019410/000201941026000016/cai-20251231.htm, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2019410/000201941026000069/cai-20260930.htm

## 4. ISRG — Intuitive Surgical

**Chokepoint:** Strong customer workflow dependence and multiple revenue nodes; the main issue is valuation and whether AI creates incremental economics beyond the existing platform moat.

**Why it can win:** Intuitive's installed systems, surgeon workflow, recurring instruments and procedure data form a durable platform. AI can enhance planning and assistance, but the current investment case remains robotic surgery rather than pure AI medicine.

**Why now:** Q3 2026 revenue grew 22%, procedure volume grew about 15%, da Vinci installed base reached 12,094 and instruments/accessories generated $1.80 billion.

**Strongest case against:** Robotic surgery remains excellent but already priced for sustained dominance, while AI adds little incremental revenue and competition compresses returns.

**We are wrong if:**
- Procedure growth falls below installed-base growth
- Recurring instrument growth weakens materially
- Competing systems reduce placements or utilization

Sources: https://isrg.intuitive.com/news-releases/news-release-details/intuitive-announces-third-quarter-earnings-6, https://isrg.intuitive.com/static-files/42b1fa2c-4c6e-4dd7-90b9-acceda8baded, https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices/list-artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices

## 6. RDNT — RadNet / DeepHealth

**Chokepoint:** Controls multiple connected nodes—care delivery, workflow, data and AI software—but Digital Health is still a small share of total revenue and must prove independent economics.

**Why it can win:** RadNet links real imaging centers, patient workflow, image data and DeepHealth software. That vertical loop may create a more defensible learning and distribution advantage than a standalone radiology algorithm vendor.

**Why now:** Q3 2026 total revenue grew 22.1%; Digital Health revenue grew 32.6% to $32.9 million, while the FDA mapping identified nine DeepHealth/Aidence records.

**Strongest case against:** The imaging-center business drives most value while DeepHealth remains a costly feature set with weak external pricing power.

**We are wrong if:**
- Digital Health growth falls below core imaging growth
- Segment cash flow remains negative after integration
- External customers do not expand beyond RadNet's own network

Sources: https://www.radnet.com/newsroom/press-releases/q3-2026-results, https://www.radnet.com/about/history, https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices/list-artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices

## 1. CBLL — Ceribell

**Chokepoint:** A clinical-workflow chokepoint in the ICU and emergency room: rapid EEG where hospitals often have no neurologist on site.

**Why it can win:** A pure-play AI brain-monitoring platform with first-in-class FDA clearances: the first device cleared to help diagnose status epilepticus and the only cleared delirium monitor, backed by a Medicare add-on payment. It controls hardware, algorithm, manufacturing and distribution.

**Why now:** Q3 2026 revenue grew 30% with gross margin near 90%.

**Strongest case against:** Losses are widening and headbands are assembled by contract manufacturers in China and Vietnam; more dilution is likely.

**We are wrong if:**
- Quarterly net loss keeps widening for two more quarters
- Revenue growth falls below 20%
- NTAP payment is not renewed

Sources: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1861107/000119312526067238/cbll-20251231.htm, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1861107/000119312526445555/cbll-20260930.htm

## 3. HTFL — HeartFlow

**Chokepoint:** A payment chokepoint: the scarce asset is the billing code, coverage and clinical evidence, not the algorithm alone.

**Why it can win:** The closest thing to a reimbursement chokepoint in AI medicine: its AI coronary analysis (FFR-CT) is paid under a Category I CPT code, insurers' coverage policies for it apply to about 99% of insured US lives, and it is written into cardiology guidelines. Its second product, plaque analysis, got its own Category I code in 2026 and is covered for about 75%. Every CT scanner and hospital that adopts it makes it harder to displace.

**Why now:** Q3 2026 revenue grew 57% with an 83.6% gross margin, and more than 1,800 institutions have adopted it.

**Strongest case against:** At roughly fifteen times sales the price already assumes years of dominance; scanner makers or cheaper AI rivals could substitute the analysis.

**We are wrong if:**
- Revenue growth falls below 25% within four quarters
- A scanner maker bundles an equivalent reimbursed analysis
- CMS cuts the CPT 75580 payment rate

Sources: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1464521/000146452126000042/htfl-20251231x10k.htm, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001464521/000146452126000171/htfl-20261112xex99_1.htm

## 5. SDGR — Schrodinger

**Chokepoint:** A horizontal discovery layer used across multiple customers and drug programs; more diversified than betting on one molecule.

**Why it can win:** A picks-and-shovels approach to AI medicine: pharma customers use its physics-based and AI software across many programs, while its proprietary pipeline preserves additional upside.

**Why now:** Software ACV growth, pharmaceutical collaborations and continuing product development provide commercial evidence outside any one clinical asset.

**Strongest case against:** Pharma customers may build comparable tools internally, hosted adoption may pressure economics, and proprietary drug programs may consume capital without validating the software advantage.

**We are wrong if:**
- Software ACV or retention weakens for two consecutive reported periods
- Software gross margin fails to recover during the hosted transition
- Major pharmaceutical collaborations contract without replacement

Sources: https://ir.schrodinger.com/press-releases/news-details/2026/Schrdinger-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/, https://ir.schrodinger.com/press-releases/news-details/2026/Schrdinger-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Financial-Results/default.aspx, https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05544019, https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05961839
